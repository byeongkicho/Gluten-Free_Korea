If you've read [the main guide](/blog/celiac-travel-korea-guide), you already know the golden rule: in Korea, "gluten-free" usually means "low-gluten" until a person you can talk to proves otherwise. This post is the companion to that guide — the full Seoul restaurant list, sorted not alphabetically but by **how much we actually trust each kitchen.**

A quick honesty note before the list: my wife has non-celiac gluten sensitivity (NCGS), so when I say a place is "fine," I mean fine for her. Celiac readers should reconfirm everything I flag and then some. Staff turnover is real, menus change, and a safe dish in March can quietly get a wheat-based sauce by August. Ask every single visit.

## How this list is tiered

- **Tier 1 — Dedicated GF.** Everything on the menu is gluten-free. You can relax and just order. There are only a handful of these in all of Korea.
- **Tier 2 — GF-friendly.** Not gluten-free overall, but the kitchen understands the request and has specific items that are safe. You order carefully and you ask every time.

Each restaurant links to its full page — address in Korean and English (hand it to a taxi driver), map, and the exact phrases to show staff.

## Tier 1 — Dedicated gluten-free

### 237 Pizza — Hannam, Yongsan-gu

The anchor of gluten-free Seoul. [237 Pizza](/place/237-pizza) is the only GFCO-certified gluten-free pizza we've been able to confirm in Korea, and the owner has celiac disease himself — he opened it out of frustration that nobody else had. The crust isn't apologetic; it's a real pizza that happens to be gluten-free, not the other way around. Made to order, so check the current wait when you arrive. This is the one place where we don't rehearse a single phrase — we just eat.

### Monil2 House — Yeonnam, Mapo-gu

[Monil2 House](/place/monil2-house) is a dedicated GF bakery-and-cafe rather than a sit-down restaurant, but it earns a spot here because it's one of the very few kitchens in Seoul where the *entire* menu is safe. The bread has real structure — most "GF bread" in Seoul is closer to rice-flour cake. A short walk from Hongik Station. (More on GF bakeries in the dedicated [bakeries & cafes guide](/blog/gluten-free-bakeries-cafes-seoul).)

## Tier 2 — GF-friendly standouts

These are not dedicated GF kitchens, but each has a real, verifiable gluten-free path. Order from the safe items, and confirm handling every visit.

### Vegetus — Haebangchon, Yongsan-gu

[Vegetus](/place/vegetus) is a 100% vegan restaurant that actually **labels gluten-free (and nut-free) dishes on the menu** — a rarity in Seoul. Confirmed GF options include the burrito bowl, wraps, pasta, lime pie, carrot cake, and brownies. It's not a dedicated GF facility, so cross-contamination is still possible; ask about shared prep if you're celiac.

<!-- KI: 방문했다면 어떤 메뉴 먹었는지 / 직원 GF 이해도 어땠는지 1인칭으로. -->

### Cucciolo Seoul — Samgakji, Yongsan-gu

[Cucciolo Seoul](/place/cucciolo-seoul) is an Italian restaurant by the Cucciolo Group inside the Amore Pacific building — a step up for a proper sit-down meal. Italian menus can be navigated gluten-free (grilled fish, risotto, salads), but pasta and fritti are the obvious traps, so verify current GF options and kitchen handling with staff. A nicer-occasion pick than most of this list.

<!-- KI: 방문했다면 GF로 실제 주문 가능했던 메뉴 / 주방 대응 노트. -->

### Grain Seoul — Sinchon, Seodaemun-gu

[Grain Seoul](/place/grain-seoul) does gluten-free brunch, including pancakes, and the staff are noticeably attentive to allergies. Second floor, no elevator. Confirm the specific items and cross-contact handling before ordering — but this is one of the easier GF brunches in the city.

<!-- KI: 방문했다면 팬케이크/브런치 실제 경험. -->

### Francois — Seokchon, Songpa-gu

[Francois](/place/francois) is a French restaurant in Songpa. French cooking has naturally gluten-free corners (proteins, vegetables, some sauces) but also flour-thickened classics, so this one leans on a good conversation with the kitchen. Verify GF options and handling on arrival.

## Fried chicken, done with rice flour

Korean fried chicken is usually a wheat-batter minefield — but two Seoul shops build the whole concept around rice flour.

### 6Day Chicken — Changsin, Jongno-gu

[6Day Chicken](/place/6day-chicken) explicitly markets "gluten-free" fried chicken. Rice-flour batter is the draw, but reconfirm which menu items qualify, what's in the seasoning powders and sauces, and whether the frying oil is shared with any wheat-battered items.

### Ssal Tongdak (Rice Fried Chicken) — Dangsan, Yeongdeungpo-gu

[Ssal Tongdak](/place/ssal-tongdak-rice-fried-chicken-dangsan) — literally "rice chicken" — is a rice-based fried chicken franchise. Plain fried chicken is typically GF, but the sauced versions (soy, gochujang, teriyaki-style) usually are not. Some branches are delivery-only, so check before you travel.

## The Korean-cuisine wildcard

### Jihwaja — Cheongun-dong, Jongno-gu

[Jihwaja](/place/jihwaja) is a traditional Korean restaurant — the highest-effort entry here. Korean set meals hide wheat in soy sauce, jeon (pan-fried) batter, and countless banchan side dishes, so a full course requires careful, item-by-item verification with staff. Included because a good hanjeongsik experience is worth the effort if you get the communication right.

## Cluster your day by neighborhood

Seoul is big and traffic is real, so it helps to group these by area rather than chasing one spot across town:

- **Yongsan-gu (west-central):** the densest GF cluster — 237 Pizza (Hannam), Vegetus (Haebangchon), and Cucciolo (Samgakji) are all within a short taxi ride of each other. A strong anchor day: lunch at 237, coffee and dessert nearby, dinner at Cucciolo.
- **Mapo / Hongik (northwest):** Monil2 House in Yeonnam, easy to pair with the bakeries in the [cafes guide](/blog/gluten-free-bakeries-cafes-seoul).
- **Jongno (historic center):** 6Day Chicken (Changsin) and Jihwaja (Cheongun-dong) — good to combine with palace sightseeing.
- **Southwest:** Ssal Tongdak (Dangsan) and Francois (Songpa) sit on opposite sides of the river; plan one, not both, in a day.

## How to actually order

The pattern that works everywhere on this list:

1. Show the allergy phrase (full version and exact wording in our [Korean phrases guide](/blog/korean-gluten-free-phrases)).
2. Name the specific hidden sources — soy sauce (간장), batter (튀김), and shared fryers — rather than just saying "no gluten."
3. Reconfirm when the food arrives, not only when you order.

## Honest limits

None of these are a guarantee for a diagnosed celiac. Dedicated GF (Tier 1) is as close as Seoul gets to relaxed dining; everything in Tier 2 depends on the kitchen getting your request right *that day*. When in doubt, anchor your trip around the Tier 1 spots and treat the rest as good options you verify in person.
