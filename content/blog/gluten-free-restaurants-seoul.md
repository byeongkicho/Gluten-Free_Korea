Seven places in Seoul with a real gluten-free path: **Monil2 House, Vegetus, Cucciolo Seoul, Grain Seoul, 6Day Chicken, Ssal Tongdak and Jihwaja.** Only one of them — Monil2 House — is a dedicated gluten-free kitchen you can order in without negotiating.

Before the list, be clear about what this is. In Korea "gluten-free" usually means "low-gluten" until a person you can talk to proves otherwise, and this list is sorted by **how strong the evidence is**, not by how good the food is. We have eaten at five of the seven. Four of those carry the month; the fifth we can date no closer than "we went". The two we have not been to are marked as such, and we would rather say so than let you assume.

My wife has non-celiac gluten sensitivity (NCGS), so when I say a place worked, I mean it worked for her. Celiac readers should reconfirm everything and then some. Staff turnover is real, menus change, and a safe dish in March can quietly get a wheat-based sauce by August. Ask every single visit.

## How this list is tiered

- **Tier 1 — Dedicated GF.** The kitchen works without wheat, so you are not picking safe items off a wheat menu. Across the 24 places we track nationwide, seven are dedicated gluten-free kitchens, and one of those — 237 Pizza — is currently closed. Of the five still open in Seoul and Gyeonggi, most are dessert cafes; Monil2 House is the one you can build a meal around. One caution that applies even here: Korea's mandatory allergen line covers **wheat but not barley or rye**, so a shop can be genuinely wheat-free and still use barley, malt or 보리차 without being required to declare it. The list of substances that must be declared is fixed by [시행규칙 별표 2](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsBylInfoPLinkR.do?lsiSeq=267855&bylNo=0002&bylBrNo=00&bylCls=BE&bylClsCd=BE) (총리령 제2004호, in force 1 January 2026); wheat is on it, barley and rye are not. If you have celiac disease, ask about 보리 and 맥아 by name — a "no wheat" answer does not cover them. Our [label-reading guide](/blog/reading-korean-food-labels) walks through a real label.
- **Tier 2 — GF-friendly.** Not gluten-free overall, but the shop advertises or lists specific gluten-free items. You order carefully and you ask every time.

Each entry opens with where its claim comes from — a visit of ours, with the month, or an unvisited listing — so you can weigh it yourself.

Each restaurant links to its full page — address in Korean and English (hand it to a taxi driver), map, and the exact phrases to show staff.

## Tier 1 — Dedicated gluten-free

### Monil2 House — Yeonnam, Mapo-gu

**Visited repeatedly — June, July and September 2025, then June and July 2026.** [Monil2 House](/place/monil2-house) is a dedicated GF bakery-and-cafe rather than a sit-down restaurant, but with 237 Pizza closed it is the anchor of this list. It is gluten-free, not merely wheat-free — that is our own conclusion from going back five times over a year, not a certificate, and no kitchen is a guarantee. Ask about 보리 and 맥아 on the day like anywhere else. The bread has real structure; most "GF bread" in Seoul is closer to rice-flour cake. A short walk from Hongik Station. (More on GF bakeries in the dedicated [bakeries & cafes guide](/blog/gluten-free-bakeries-cafes-seoul).)

#### 237 Pizza is closed — and this is what it cost us

For most of the time we've been writing this site, the answer to "where can a celiac just relax and order in Seoul" was [237 Pizza](/place/237-pizza) in Hannam: dedicated gluten-free, owner with celiac disease, and the only GFCO-certified gluten-free pizza we have been able to confirm anywhere in Korea. We photographed the certification mark on its in-store menu in June 2025, next to the bag of Caputo Fioreglut it bakes with.

It has been shut since summer 2026. It was closed for remodelling when we last went, its website ([237pizza.com](https://www.237pizza.com/)) has been returning a server error since August 2026 — still HTTP 500 when we checked on 8 September 2026 — and a [Korean blogger writing on 3 September 2026](https://blog.naver.com/thddhgus/224399729002) opens a review by describing the Hannam closure while hunting for a replacement (they found one: Viva Napoli near Magok station, which does gluten-free pizza without being a dedicated shop). We have not reached anyone who can say whether it is coming back.

We are leaving it here rather than deleting it, because one shop closing is the honest shape of this list: Seoul has a handful of dedicated gluten-free kitchens, and losing one moves the whole city. **If you walk past and find it open, tell us** — contact@noglutenkorea.com or [@noglutenkorea](https://instagram.com/noglutenkorea) — and we will put it back the same day.

## Tier 2 — GF-friendly standouts

These are not dedicated GF kitchens, but each has a real, verifiable gluten-free path. Order from the safe items, and confirm handling every visit.

### Vegetus — Haebangchon, Yongsan-gu

**Not visited — listed on the shop's own menu, checked September 2026.** [Vegetus](/place/vegetus) is a 100% vegan restaurant that actually **labels gluten-free (and nut-free) dishes on the menu** — a rarity in Seoul. The menu marks the burrito bowl, wraps, pasta, lime pie, carrot cake and brownies as gluten-free. That is the shop's label, not our test — we have not eaten there, and reading a menu is not checking an ingredient. It's not a dedicated GF facility, so cross-contamination is still possible; ask about shared prep if you're celiac.

### Cucciolo Seoul — Samgakji, Yongsan-gu

**Visited July 2025.** [Cucciolo Seoul](/place/cucciolo-seoul) is an Italian restaurant by the Cucciolo Group inside the Amore Pacific building — a step up for a proper sit-down meal. Italian menus can be navigated gluten-free (grilled fish, risotto, salads), but pasta and fritti are the obvious traps, so verify current GF options and kitchen handling with staff. A nicer-occasion pick than most of this list.

### Grain Seoul — Sinchon, Seodaemun-gu

**Visited January and March 2026.** [Grain Seoul](/place/grain-seoul) does gluten-free brunch, including pancakes, and the staff are noticeably attentive to allergies. Second floor, no elevator. Confirm the specific items and cross-contact handling before ordering — but this is one of the easier GF brunches in the city.

## Fried chicken, done with rice flour

Korean fried chicken is usually a wheat-batter minefield — but two Seoul shops build the whole concept around rice flour.

### 6Day Chicken — Changsin, Jongno-gu

**Visited November 2025.** [6Day Chicken](/place/6day-chicken) explicitly markets "gluten-free" fried chicken. Rice-flour batter is the draw, but reconfirm which menu items qualify, what's in the seasoning powders and sauces, and whether the frying oil is shared with any wheat-battered items.

### Ssal Tongdak (Rice Fried Chicken) — Dangsan, Yeongdeungpo-gu

**Visited, month not recorded.** [Ssal Tongdak](/place/ssal-tongdak-rice-fried-chicken-dangsan) — 쌀통닭, "rice whole-chicken" — is a rice-based fried chicken franchise. Plain fried chicken is typically GF, but the sauced versions (soy, gochujang, teriyaki-style) usually are not. Ask the same fryer question as at 6Day Chicken: rice batter does not help if the oil is shared with wheat-battered items, and franchise branches set their own kitchen practice. Some are delivery-only, so check before you travel.

## The Korean-cuisine wildcard

### Jihwaja — Cheongun-dong, Jongno-gu

**Not visited — but the menu carries a gluten-free course.** That is what earns it a place here rather than a general hope; a set course is easier to make safe than an à la carte order, because the kitchen plans the whole sequence. [Jihwaja](/place/jihwaja) is a traditional Korean restaurant, and the highest-effort entry on this list. Korean set meals hide wheat in soy sauce, jeon (pan-fried) batter, and countless banchan side dishes, so a full course requires careful, item-by-item verification with staff. Included because a good hanjeongsik experience is worth the effort if you get the communication right.

## Cluster your day by neighborhood

Seoul is big and traffic is real, so it helps to group these by area rather than chasing one spot across town:

- **Mapo / Hongik (northwest):** Monil2 House in Yeonnam is the one Tier 1 stop left, and it pairs easily with the bakeries in the [cafes guide](/blog/gluten-free-bakeries-cafes-seoul). If you only get one relaxed meal in Seoul, spend it here.
- **Yongsan-gu (west-central):** still the densest Tier 2 cluster — Vegetus (Haebangchon) and Cucciolo (Samgakji) are a short taxi ride apart. This was a stronger day when 237 Pizza was open in Hannam.
- **Jongno (historic center):** 6Day Chicken (Changsin) and Jihwaja (Cheongun-dong) — good to combine with palace sightseeing.
- **Southwest:** Ssal Tongdak (Dangsan) is the one stop out this way; it does not pair well with anything else on the list in a single day.

## How to actually order

The pattern that works everywhere on this list:

1. Show the allergy phrase (full version and exact wording in our [Korean phrases guide](/blog/korean-gluten-free-phrases)).
2. Name the specific hidden sources — soy sauce (간장), batter (튀김), and shared fryers — rather than just saying "no gluten."
3. Reconfirm when the food arrives, not only when you order.

## Honest limits

None of these are a guarantee for a diagnosed celiac. Dedicated GF (Tier 1) is as close as Seoul gets to relaxed dining; everything in Tier 2 depends on the kitchen getting your request right *that day*. With 237 Pizza closed there is exactly one Tier 1 kitchen left on this list, so anchor your trip on Monil2 House and treat everything else as a good option you verify in person. That is a thinner safety net than Seoul had a year ago, and pretending otherwise would not help you.
